## 1. Overview and Purpose

Create a public, well-organised portfolio that demonstrates your learning progress—from computing and data foundations to responsible data engineering practice.

Your e-portfolio should use selected work, evidence, and reflection to explain how your understanding develops throughout SCST1133. It functions as both an assessment submission and a professional record of your learning across six key pillars:

1. **Foundations:** Computing concepts and digital tools.
2. **Landscape:** Data engineering and the data landscape.
3. **Ecosystem:** Industry roles, components, and collaboration.
4. **Lifecycle:** Data acquisition and engineering workflow.
5. **Responsibility:** Management, governance, and ethics.
6. **Innovation:** Emerging areas and design thinking.

---

## 2. Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

The portfolio must provide explicit evidence addressing all three course learning outcomes:

* **CLO1 · EXPLAIN:**  
  Explain basic information technology concepts and their role in data-driven systems using systems thinking.
* **CLO2 · CONSTRUCT:**  
  Construct a basic data engineering lifecycle using appropriate technologies, practices, and industry standards.
* **CLO3 · DEVELOP:**  
  Develop a prototype that addresses the data engineering ecosystem and lifecycle while demonstrating ethical principles and professional responsibility.

---

## 3. Required Portfolio Sections

All six sections must be included, either as separate pages or as clearly labelled sections within a unified single-page website:

### Section A: Home and Professional Profile
* Introduce yourself as a computing student interested in data engineering.
* Include programme of study, education, relevant technical skills, and interests.
* Display official UTM Faculty of Computing logo (maintain exact proportions and colours).
* Links to GitHub profile, published portfolio URL, and optional LinkedIn.
* **Privacy safeguard:** Do **NOT** publish private contact details (phone numbers, home address, passwords, tokens).

### Section B: Learning Journey
* Organise work around SCST1133 course topics and themes.
* Show connections between foundations, tools, data landscape, industry, lifecycle, acquisition, governance, ethics, and emerging fields.

### Section C: Selected Work and Evidence
* Present substantial artifacts from course activities and assessments:
  * Design Thinking and Figma prototype (problem, idea, link, screenshots, contribution).
  * Industry Talk (poster, notes/report, key lessons, connection to DE practice).
  * Industry Visit (approved photos/evidence, observations, report, professional takeaways).
  * Data Engineering Lifecycle video (video link, storyboard/slides, lifecycle decisions).
  * Data acquisition work (dataset, collection method, tools, data quality, output).
  * Project presentations / other selected activities (slides, demo, external links).

### Section D: Reflection on Artifacts
* Detailed reflection for each major artifact (not just a descriptive summary of steps).
* Address contribution, technical concept applied, challenge/decision encountered, connection to CLOs, and future improvements.

### Section E: Course Learning Summary
* Synthesise knowledge and skills acquired throughout the semester.
* Articulate how the course shifted your perspective on data engineering.
* Identify future learning goals and areas for further development.

### Section F: Sources and Acknowledgements
* Full academic citations and credits for external data, images, code, text, and templates.
* Explicit statement detailing when and how digital or AI tools supported your work.

## 5. Artifact Reflection Framework

Reflections must be analytical rather than merely descriptive. Structure artifact reflections around these five core questions:

1. **Contribution and Purpose:** What did I create or contribute, and what was its purpose?
2. **Learning and Application:** What data engineering concept, tool, or practice did I learn or apply?
3. **Challenge and Decision:** What challenge or decision affected my work, and how did I respond?
4. **CLO Evidence:** How does this work demonstrate one or more course learning outcomes (CLO1, CLO2, or CLO3)?
5. **Next Improvement:** What would I change, improve, or investigate next?

### Example Model
> *Artifact:* Data Engineering Lifecycle Video · CLO2  
> *"Preparing the lifecycle video helped me understand how data acquisition, storage and transformation connect. I found it challenging to explain where validation should take place. If I revised the video, I would add a clear example showing how a data quality check affects the next stage."*

## 6. Assessment Rubric (100 Marks Total)

| Criterion | Marks | Meets the criterion strongly | Needs improvement |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Course evidence and completeness** | **25** | Selected artifacts are substantial, clearly explained, and cover the course learning journey. | Evidence is limited, unclear, incomplete, or weakly connected to SCST1133. |
| **Reflection and analysis** | **25** | Reflections explain learning, decisions, and challenges, and identify specific improvements. | Reflections are missing, mostly descriptive, or provide little analysis of learning. |
| **Understanding and CLO alignment** | **20** | Explanations are accurate and explicitly connect work to data engineering concepts and relevant CLOs. | Explanations include inaccuracies or do not show how work relates to course concepts or CLOs. |
| **Organisation and navigation** | **10** | Sections are easy to find, links work, and content has a logical flow. | Navigation is confusing, links are broken, or the structure makes evidence difficult to locate. |
| **Visual and communication quality** | **10** | The design is readable, consistent, and suitable for a professional learning portfolio. | The design or writing makes the portfolio difficult to read or understand. |
| **Professionalism and academic integrity** | **10** | Work is original, sources and tool use are acknowledged, and privacy is protected. | Sources or tool use are not acknowledged, copied work appears, or privacy is not handled responsibly. |

*Course-weight conversion:* $\text{Portfolio Mark} \div 100 \times 10$ (e.g., $85/100 = 8.5\%$ towards final grade).
